Army · 11B

Infantryman

Infantry is far more than a path to police or security work. It builds decision-making under pressure, small-unit leadership, mission planning, communications, land navigation, weapons safety, and first aid. Those are transferable leadership and operations capabilities that open doors in trades, operations, public safety, training, and government, not a single career lane.

Small-unit leadership and decision-making under stressMission planning, briefing, and executionLand navigation and situational awarenessWeapons handling and range safetyCommunications, reporting, and accountabilityTactical combat casualty care (first aid under pressure)

Your rank changes the story

Use the band that reflects what you actually did—not rank alone. An E-2 and an E-7 in the same code should not submit the same résumé.

01

E-1–E-4

Lead with individual and team tasks, weapons and equipment qualifications, first-aid training, physical standards, and the conditions you operated in. Do not inflate supervisory scope.

02

E-5–E-6 / NCO

Add team/squad leadership: soldiers led, training delivered, mission planning, standards enforcement, and accountability for people and sensitive equipment.

03

E-7–E-9 / Senior NCO

Emphasize platoon/company operations, planning and rehearsals, personnel development, resource and readiness management, and advising senior leaders.

7 directions worth exploring

Some are direct transfers; others need civilian credentials. The point is to widen the map while staying honest about qualification gaps.

01
Civilian

Skilled trades apprenticeship

Discipline, teamwork, and working safely in tough conditions transfer well to construction and industrial trades.

Bridge: Enter a registered/union apprenticeship (e.g., Helmets to Hardhats); no trade license is implied by service.

Civilian

Operations / production supervisor

Leading teams, enforcing standards, and hitting objectives maps to frontline operations leadership.

Bridge: Translate team size, tempo, and outcomes into civilian terms; Lean fundamentals help.

Emergency medical or fire services

Casualty care, composure, and teamwork support EMS and fire work.

Bridge: Earn EMT or firefighter certification; state licensure is separate from military training.

One credible option among many, not the default; leadership and judgment apply.

Build evidence, not just a translated title

Hands-on scope

List systems, tools, vehicles, environments, certifications, and recurring tasks.

Leadership scope

State people trained or supervised, asset value, work volume, readiness, safety, and quality outcomes.

Credential gap

Identify licenses, degrees, software, or civilian standards required before claiming the target role.

Search wider

Search by capabilities and equipment as well as by “11B” or “Infantryman.”

Reality check: MOS, AFSC, rating, or rank never guarantees civilian qualification. Duties vary by unit and assignment; regulated professions require their own education, experience, exams, or licenses.
