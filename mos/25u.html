Army · 25U

Signal Support Systems Specialist

Signal support specialists install and maintain communications and network equipment, radios, satellite, and COMSEC systems, plus user support and field repair. That maps directly to IT, network, telecom, field-service, and cybersecurity directions with recognized certification bridges.

Your rank changes the story

Use the band that reflects what you actually did—not rank alone. An E-2 and an E-7 in the same code should not submit the same résumé.

01

E-1–E-4

Lead with equipment installed/maintained, systems, certifications, and conditions.

02

E-5–E-6 / NCO

Add team leadership, project support, training, and the networks you managed.

03

E-7–E-9 / Senior NCO

Emphasize communications operations, planning, and organization-level results.

Build evidence, not just a translated title

Hands-on scope

List systems, tools, vehicles, environments, certifications, and recurring tasks.

Leadership scope

State people trained or supervised, asset value, work volume, readiness, safety, and quality outcomes.

Credential gap

Identify licenses, degrees, software, or civilian standards required before claiming the target role.

Search wider

Search by capabilities and equipment as well as by “25U” or “Signal Support Systems Specialist.”

Reality check: MOS, AFSC, rating, or rank never guarantees civilian qualification. Duties vary by unit and assignment; regulated professions require their own education, experience, exams, or licenses.
