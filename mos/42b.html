Your rank changes the story

Use the level that reflects what you actually did—not rank alone. Two people with the same code and very different experience should not submit the same résumé.

O-1–O-3

Company-Grade Officer

Translate planning, technical judgment, briefings, team leadership, and the people and resources you were accountable for.

O-4–O-5

Field-Grade Officer

Emphasize programs, budgets, cross-functional organizations, policy, risk ownership, and measurable operational outcomes.

O-6+

Senior Officer

Target director, program, operations, and public-sector leadership roles that match your actual scope.

Turn 42B into proof employers trust

A translated title isn't enough. These four moves turn Human Resources Officer experience into evidence a civilian hiring manager can act on.

  1. Show what you ran

    List the systems, vehicles, equipment, and tools you personally operated as Human Resources Officer—plus qualifications, certifications, and the recurring high-stakes tasks you owned.

  2. Put numbers on leadership

    Soldiers led, dollar value of equipment you were accountable for, workload, readiness rates, and safety or quality outcomes. Scope beats adjectives.

  3. Close the credential gap early

    For any target role, name the license, degree, or software it requires and start now—your GI Bill and credentialing assistance can cover most of it.

  4. Search by capability, not code

    Recruiters rarely search “42B.” Search job boards by the equipment, skills, and outcomes above as much as by “Human Resources Officer.”

How to use this: Treat these as targets, not limits. Your exact duties depend on your assignments, so lead with what you actually did—and remember that regulated careers require their own license, certification, or degree.
