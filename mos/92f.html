Army · 92F

Petroleum Supply Specialist

Petroleum supply specialists store, distribute, and test fuels, handle hazardous materials safely, operate and maintain equipment, and maintain accountability. That maps to energy and fuel operations, hazmat transport, quality/lab, safety and environmental, logistics, and federal directions.

Your rank changes the story

Use the level that reflects what you actually did—not rank alone. Two people with the same code and very different experience should not submit the same résumé.

E-1–E-4

Junior Enlisted

Lead with volumes handled, systems, testing, hazmat, and safety.

E-5–E-6

Non-Commissioned Officer

Add team leadership, safety oversight, training, and accountability outcomes.

E-7–E-9

Senior NCO

Emphasize fuel operations, safety/environmental programs, and resource management.

Turn 92F into proof employers trust

A translated title isn't enough. These four moves turn Petroleum Supply Specialist experience into evidence a civilian hiring manager can act on.

  1. Show what you ran

    List the systems, vehicles, equipment, and tools you personally operated as Petroleum Supply Specialist—plus qualifications, certifications, and the recurring high-stakes tasks you owned.

  2. Put numbers on leadership

    Soldiers led, dollar value of equipment you were accountable for, workload, readiness rates, and safety or quality outcomes. Scope beats adjectives.

  3. Close the credential gap early

    For any target role, name the license, degree, or software it requires and start now—your GI Bill and credentialing assistance can cover most of it.

  4. Search by capability, not code

    Recruiters rarely search “92F.” Search job boards by the equipment, skills, and outcomes above as much as by “Petroleum Supply Specialist.”

Programs built to hire & train veterans

You don't have to make this jump alone. These programs exist specifically to move service members into civilian careers—most are free, and several pay you while you train.

DoD SkillBridge ↗

Train or intern with a civilian employer during your last ~180 days on active duty—at full military pay.

Registered Apprenticeships ↗

Earn a paycheck while you train for a trade or technical role; USMAP can credit the military training you already have.

Hiring Our Heroes ↗

Corporate fellowships and hiring events that place transitioning service members and military spouses.

Onward to Opportunity ↗

Free career training and nationally recognized certifications for service members, veterans, and spouses.

VA VET TEC ↗

VA-funded high-tech training (IT, cyber, data, software) that does not touch your GI Bill entitlement.

CareerOneStop (U.S. DOL) ↗

Free local career centers plus certification and state-by-state license finders for any field.

Amazon military roles ↗

Logistics, operations, and driver pathways that actively recruit veterans.

How to use this: Treat these as targets, not limits. Your exact duties depend on your assignments, so lead with what you actually did—and remember that regulated careers require their own license, certification, or degree.
